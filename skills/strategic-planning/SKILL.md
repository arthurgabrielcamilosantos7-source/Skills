# Strategic planning

A plan is useful when it says what to do first, how to know it worked, and what could stop it.

## 1. Pin down the goal
State the goal as an outcome with a measure and a date ("10 paying clients by March", not "grow the business"). Ask for constraints: budget, hours per week, people, skills, deadlines, things that are off-limits. If the user cannot answer, propose a goal and mark it as an assumption.

## 2. Understand the situation
Briefly: where things stand today, what has been tried, the resources available, and the main obstacle. Separate facts from assumptions. If missing facts matter, research them (use source-verification for key numbers) or list them as open questions.

## 3. Choose the strategy
Generate 2-3 genuinely different approaches. For each: why it could work, cost, time to first result, main risk. Recommend one and say why; do not hedge between all of them.

## 4. Build the plan
- **Milestones**: 3-6 checkpoints, each with a measurable result and a target date.
- **Next actions**: for the first milestone, concrete tasks of a day or less, in order, with an owner if there is a team.
- **Priorities**: rank by impact versus effort; do high-impact, low-effort items first, and drop or postpone low-impact work explicitly.
- **Dependencies**: what must happen before what.

## 5. Risks and checkpoints
List the top 3-5 risks with likelihood, impact, and a mitigation or early warning sign. Set the review rhythm (weekly or per milestone) and the metric that means "change course".

## Output
Keep it to about one page: goal, recommended strategy (alternatives in one line each), milestones table, next 5-10 actions, risks, review rhythm. Write in the user's language. Offer to turn it into a document, a spreadsheet tracker or tickets if that would help.
